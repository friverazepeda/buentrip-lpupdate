Highlights

  • Yumari secured a significant order of $490,000 in Q1 from Creative Voice, with an expectation of this becoming an annual $2 million client. Cuts Clothing, a company that made 15 million shirts last year, has approved samples and wants Yumari to be their primary supplier, starting with 50,000 garments monthly, generating $35,000. The internal AI agent 'Yumi' has significantly improved operational efficiency, reducing the team from five to two people and handling tasks like document compliance and product development. This efficiency has led to the development of new SaaS products, 'Fibra' for brands and 'Hilo' for factories, aiming for monthly recurring revenue. The company is projected to reach profitability by the end of Q2 (June) due to the Cuts Clothing contract.

Asks

  • The investor asks for clarification on the unit economics, particularly the negative margin, and the gross margins. They also seek an update on the company's runway and the progress of the funding round, including committed funds and potential investors. There's an implicit ask for the company to consider investor advice on focusing on the core business before launching new products.

Risks

  • The initial unit economics presented in the DEC were confusing, showing a negative margin. There's a challenge in balancing the existing transactional business model with the new AI agent SaaS product development, with concerns about expanding too quickly and diverting focus from the core business. The apparel manufacturing industry in Latam faces challenges such as unfair competition from Asia, lack of professionalization and digitalization in factories, poor customer service, and slow sample production. The current funding round has commitments, but the valuation is a concern for some smaller funds due to their model.
