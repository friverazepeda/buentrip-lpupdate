Summary

Rampa is launching eBOB, a stablecoin version of the Boliviano, with institutional clients like Binance, Vita Wallet, and Takenos. The company plans to expand to Ecuador and other Latin American countries, aiming for an exit strategy through acquisition by a larger stablecoin issuer. Gustavo, the founder, is transitioning to a role at IFC while remaining involved as CFO, and Igor has joined as CEO to lead the stablecoin initiative and product improvements for Rampa's cross-border payments.

Highlights

  • Igor has joined Rampa as CEO to lead the stablecoin initiative. Rampa is launching eBOB, the first Bolivian token, with access to central bank APIs and existing local payments infrastructure. The eBOB will be distributed to major clients like Binance, Vita Wallet, and Takenos. Rampa plans to expand its operations to Ecuador, with Daniela Espinosa leading the effort, and potentially to Peru and Paraguay. Gustavo has accepted a role at IFC (World Bank) to lead private sector operations in Bolivia, which he believes will also benefit Rampa through connections and leverage. The eBOB project is seen as a potential exit strategy for Rampa, aiming for acquisition by companies like Theta or Circle.

Risks

  • The regulatory environment in Ecuador for fintech and digital currencies is contradictory, with strong regulatory capture by banks making it difficult for new fintechs to operate. There is a historical opposition in Ecuador to government-issued digital currency, which could pose challenges for the e-dollar initiative. The high yield on Bolivian treasuries (13-15%) indicates scarce liquidity, which could impact the stablecoin's underlying asset strategy. The behavior of customers regarding how long they hold the e-BOB and the dynamic movement of fiat conversions are unknowns. ASFI's (Bolivian financial regulator) stance on the e-BOB, while currently positive, remains an unknown risk. Gustavo's new role at IFC means he cannot be a public figure for Rampa, requiring a strategic approach to public communication and potentially a new public face for eBOB. Igor's limited Spanish proficiency could be a hindrance in local interactions, though a team is in place to support this.
